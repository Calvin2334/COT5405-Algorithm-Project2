This report applies greedy and divide-and-conquer
algorithms to two problems from traffic signal
operations: selecting a maximum set of compatible
green-light windows (Section~II) and identifying the
period of worst queue build-up on an intersection

\section{Conclusion}

We modeled two traffic signal problems as classical
algorithmic problems: selecting compatible green-light
windows as interval scheduling, solved optimally by an
earliest-finish-time greedy algorithm in $O(n\log n)$
time, and locating the worst queue build-up as a
maximum subarray, solved by divide and conquer in
$\Theta(n\log n)$ time. Both algorithms were proved
correct, and C++ experiments confirmed the predicted
growth rates. The models are deliberately simplified,
but they show how standard algorithmic techniques can
give provably correct answers to concrete
traffic-operations questions.
