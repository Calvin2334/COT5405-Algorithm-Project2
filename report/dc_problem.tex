\subsection{Real-World Problem Description}

Signalized intersections are typically retimed based on
when and how severely queues build up on their
approaches. Traffic engineers study queues with
input--output (cumulative arrival and departure)
analysis: the queue on an approach grows when more
vehicles arrive than the signal discharges, and shrinks
otherwise \cite{daganzo1997, hcm2022}. Detectors report,
for every short time bin (e.g., one minute), how many
vehicles arrived at and departed through the stop line,
producing a long sequence of per-minute counts.

An engineer asks: \emph{during which continuous period
did the queue on this approach grow the most?} This
worst congestion episode shows when the current timing
is least able to serve demand and is a candidate for
retiming or coordination \cite{fhwa2008}. The period
may start and end at any minute and may contain short
intervals in which the queue partially drains, as long
as the overall build-up is maximal. We assume accurate
counts, a single approach studied independently,
equal-length time bins, and no physical storage limit;
the model identifies the worst episode but does not
itself prescribe a new signal plan.

\subsection{Mathematical Abstraction}

For each of $n$ consecutive minutes, let $a_i$ and $d_i$
be the arrivals and departures; the net queue change is
$x_i = a_i - d_i \in \mathbb{Z}$, giving a sequence
$X = \langle x_1, \ldots, x_n \rangle$ ($x_i > 0$: the
queue grew; $x_i < 0$: it drained). The candidate
periods form the set
\[
\mathcal{I}_n = \{ [l, r] : 1 \le l \le r \le n \},
\qquad |\mathcal{I}_n| = n(n+1)/2,
\]
and the build-up over a period is
$S(l, r) = \sum_{k=l}^{r} x_k$, which under the
input--output model equals the change in queue length
from the start of minute $l$ to the end of minute $r$.
The goal is to find
\[
[l^*, r^*] \in \arg\max_{[l,r] \in \mathcal{I}_n} S(l, r),
\]
the \emph{maximum subarray} problem
\cite{bentley1984, clrs2009}. Periods are non-empty; if
every $x_i < 0$, the answer is the single minute with
the smallest drain.
