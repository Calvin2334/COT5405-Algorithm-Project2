\subsection{Running-Time Analysis}

\subsubsection{Theoretical Analysis}

Let $T(n)$ be the running time on $n$ minutes, with
$T(1) = \Theta(1)$. For $n \ge 2$, computing
$\mathit{mid}$ and choosing the best of three candidates
take $O(1)$ time, the recursive calls are on ranges of
length $\lceil n/2 \rceil$ and $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$, and
\textsc{Max-Crossing} visits each of the $n$ elements
once, taking $\Theta(n)$ time. Therefore
\[
T(n) = T(\lceil n/2 \rceil) + T(\lfloor n/2 \rfloor) + \Theta(n).
\]
With $a = b = 2$ and $f(n) = \Theta(n) = \Theta(n^{\log_b a})$,
case~2 of the Master Theorem \cite[Sec.~4.5]{clrs2009}
(floors and ceilings do not change the solution
\cite[Sec.~4.6]{clrs2009}) gives
$\boxed{T(n) = \Theta(n \log n)}$.

The recursion tree gives the same bound: the
subproblems at each depth are disjoint ranges of
$[1, n]$, so the crossing scans at that depth visit at
most $n$ elements, and the depth is
$\lceil \log_2 n \rceil$. When $n$ is a power of two,
the crossing loops perform exactly $n \log_2 n$
additions regardless of the values $x_i$, so the bound
holds for every input. The $2n - 1$ recursive calls add
a $\Theta(n)$ term, and the extra space is $O(\log n)$
for the recursion stack, since the array is never
copied.

\subsubsection{Experimental Verification}

The C++ implementation was run on random per-minute net
changes drawn uniformly from $-30$ to $+30$, for eleven
sizes from 10,000 to 16,000,000 minutes, taking the
median of 10 measurements (Table~\ref{tab:dc-runtime}).
Because small inputs finish faster than the clock
resolution, each measurement averages
$\max(1, \lfloor 4\cdot 10^6 / n \rfloor)$ runs. Timing
used the \texttt{chrono} steady clock, excluded input
generation, and was compiled with \texttt{g++ -O2}.
Before timing, the implementation was checked against an
$O(n^2)$ brute-force search on 2,000 random inputs of
length 1 to 60, and every benchmark answer against an
independent $O(n)$ scan \cite{bentley1984}; all checks
passed.

The normalized time rises steadily with $\log_2 n$; a
least-squares fit gives
$T(n)/n \approx 0.72\log_2 n + 17.2$ ns ($R^2 = 0.889$),
a positive $n\log n$ coefficient consistent with the
$\Theta(n\log n)$ analysis. The large intercept reflects
the cost of the recursive calls relative to the cheap
additions in the crossing loops, which is why the top
plot looks nearly linear. Deviations, such as the lower
time at $n = 10{,}000$ (where the input fits in cache)
and the fluctuation at the two largest sizes, are
attributed to memory-hierarchy effects and system
activity.
